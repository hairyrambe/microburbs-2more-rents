What we found

  1. Typical error $45 a week, with 79.6% of estimates within $100, on 3,964 held-out May-July 2026 adverts. The suburb x bedrooms median on the same adverts is $100 off (55.5% within $100), matching the task's own simple benchmarks.
  2. A flat's own past adverts are the best guide to its next rent. 75% of the 65,777 flats have been advertised before; for those our typical error is $41, against $60 for flats never advertised. The building's average rent for the same bedroom count is the next strongest signal.
  3. A car space adds about $20 a week to the typical flat once its building and history are known: $7 for studios, $20 for two bedrooms, $41 for three. It is worth most around the harbour: Dawes Point, The Rocks and Barangaroo ($95+). A raw comparison within buildings shows about 8% more for flats with parking, but much of that is bigger flats having the car spaces.
  4. Same-flat rents rose 4.5% over the year to July 2026. We rebuild a monthly repeat-rent index from flats advertised more than once and convert every past advert to July 2026 dollars before learning from it.
  5. Height matters less than expected: once the building is known, each extra floor adds only about $1.03 a week. The building, not the floor, sets the price.

How accurate is it?

Held-out check: everything (rent index, building and flat history, model) was learned from adverts listed up to 2026-04-30 and tested on 3,964 adverts listed 2026-05-01 to 2026-07-31. Estimates are made as at 2026-04-30, the same way the real submission is made as at 31 July 2026 and scored on later adverts. Adverts with unknown parking are left out, as the scorer does.

How we split adverts by date

The task is set at 31 July 2026. We drop every advert listed after that date before doing anything else (the code asserts it). For our own check we train on adverts before 2026-05-01 and score on 2026-05-01–2026-07-31, mimicking the real test, which scores later adverts. We never use the official 1 August – 30 September frozen adverts, and we never call the live for-rent listings endpoint. The final model is then refit on all adverts up to 31 July 2026.

The car-space premium

Every flat gets two estimates from the same model: one with the car-space flag set to 1, one with it set to 0. The gap is the premium. We never let a car space lower the rent.

Data

  • The supplied past-leases file: 112,457 rent adverts for City of Sydney flats, 2012 to 31 July 2026. We use those from 2015 on.
  • The supplied flats file (bedrooms, bathrooms, floor, building size, ABS block) and 2021 Census / 2026 block estimates.
  • Cleaning: 112,457 raw adverts → 112,457 on or before 31 Jul 2026 → 0 usable adverts after dropping pre-2015 adverts, implausible rents (outside $150–$6,000/week), duplicates and adverts with no bedroom count. Missing bedrooms, bathrooms and parking on an advert are filled from the flat's own record.

Method

  1. Rent index. A monthly same-flat index (log rent = flat effect + month effect, fitted on flats advertised more than once, smoothed over 3 months). Every past advert is converted to July 2026 dollars.
  2. Features. Bedrooms, bathrooms, car space, property type, suburb, latitude and longitude, floor, flats in the building, block homes and new homes; the building's average adjusted rent (overall and for the same bedrooms) and the ABS block's for the same bedrooms; and the flat's own last advertised rent, how long ago it was, and whether it had parking then. For training rows these use leave-one-out or strictly earlier adverts, so no advert sees its own rent.
  3. Model. Gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn HistGradientBoosting) on log adjusted rent with absolute-error loss, since the task is scored on median absolute error.
  4. Two answers per flat. The same model is run with the car-space flag set to 1 and to 0. A car space is never allowed to lower the estimate. Any flat the model cannot score gets its suburb × bedrooms median, so no answer is blank.
  5. Tested and rejected. A larger fixed parking premium (3–8%) and scaling estimates down 1–3% made almost no difference on our check, so we kept the model as it is.
